# Content calendar and automations

Little Lantern (YouTube) brings the audience. The Etsy shop, Tonight's Sky, Softly, makes the money.
Every YouTube description ends with a link to the Etsy shop.

## How often we release on YouTube

**Four long videos a week, always at 7pm UK time**, plus Shorts once the long videos are running smoothly.

| Day | What | Length | Made for kids? | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Monday | **Luna story**: an animated bedtime story starring Luna and her starlight animal | 8 to 10 min | Yes | The heart of the channel; it shows exactly what the Etsy gift looks like |
| Wednesday | **Tonight's Sky**: this week's real sky over the UK (Moon, planets, a star and its story) | 5 min | Yes | True and timely, nobody else does it, easy to search for ("what can I see in the sky tonight") |
| Friday | **Sleep video**: slow nebula drift with soft music, no talking after the first minute | 1 to 3 hours | No | Long sleep videos earn the most and get rewatched every night |
| Sunday | **Luna story** (a different theme from Monday's) | 8 to 10 min | Yes | Sunday is a big bedtime-routine night |
| Daily, from week 5 | **Short**: a 30 to 60 second clip from that week's videos | under 60 s | Matches its source | Shorts bring new viewers to the long videos |

Four a week is enough to grow without looking mass-produced. YouTube's rules penalise repetitive
AI content, so every upload needs its own theme, title and picture. The calendar below gives each one a
real reason to exist, mostly real sky events.
