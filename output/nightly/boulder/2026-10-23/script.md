# Tonight's Sky, Softly. Boulder: Friday 23 October 2026

Good evening, Boulder. This is Tonight's Sky, Softly. It is Friday, the twenty-third of October. Settle in. Let's find out what is shining above your roof tonight.

Let your shoulders drop. Breathe in, and breathe out. The Sun set this evening at ten past six in the evening. The sky will be properly dark by twenty to eight in the evening. Today's daylight was seventeen minutes shorter than a week ago.

The Moon. Waxing Gibbous. Nine tenths lit. High up in the south-east, in front of the stars of Pisces. It's more than half full now, and growing. Its brightness will wash out some of the fainter stars. The next full moon is in two nights. In the old almanacs it's called the Hunter's Moon.

The planets. Saturn. Bright. A little way up in the east. Saturn is a planet so light for its size that, in a big enough bath, it would float. And for anyone awake before sunrise: Mars and Jupiter will be shining in the morning sky.

The stars. Vega. Very high in the west. It's a bright blue-white star that spins so fast it is squashed into an egg shape. The light you see tonight left it around two thousand and one, and has been travelling ever since. Alkaid. A little way up in the north-west. The light you see tonight left it around nineteen twenty-five, and has been travelling ever since. Polaris. High up in the north. It's the North Star, which stays almost perfectly still while the sky turns around it. The light you see tonight set off around the year fifteen ninety-five.

Straight above your roof tonight is the constellation of Cygnus. Cygnus is the swan, flying down the Milky Way with its long neck stretched out. Its brightest stars make a shape called the Northern Cross.

And very high in the north is Cepheus. Cepheus is a king, shaped a little like a child's drawing of a house, sitting next to his queen, Cassiopeia.

Coming up in the sky. The Orionids meteor shower is fading now, but still sending the odd shooting star. The shooting stars seem to fly out from Orion, and the best time to look is late at night, from somewhere dark.

Tonight's sky fact. The Moon is about a quarter as wide as the Earth. If the Earth were a basketball, the Moon would be a tennis ball.

Now, if you can, look out of the window. Can you find the Moon and Saturn, over in the east? Take your time. Here is tonight's sky once more, slowly. Moon, waxing gibbous, south-east. Saturn, east. Vega, west. Alkaid, north-west. And that is tonight's sky. Everything up there will carry on shining, all night long, without making a sound. Goodnight. Clear skies.
